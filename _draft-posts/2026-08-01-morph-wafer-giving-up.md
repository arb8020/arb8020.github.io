morph labs and wafer ai are companies i used to work at. its not for sure but i was thinking about why i still felt uncomfortable or unresolved when thinking about them or when asked about previous companies i worked at. i think leaving a company feels like giving up on them for me. i value loyalty and growth a lot and i havent really learned yet what it really means to drift apart from someone.

the songs i was thinking of are choosin texas by ella langley, ao no sumika (the jjk season 2 hidden inventory arc intro), never really over by katy perry. the katy perry song fits most with a girl i was in a situationship (? or i overestimated our friendship) with in high school. ao no sumika was actually the inspiration for the nostalgia poem, my emotional reaction to it made me think a lot about how emotions about the past feel most salient to me. they're already gone and cant be changed. im still learning that the future and present are more similar to that than they might seem (implied by the poem). choosin texas is a more recent one. i just moved from SF to NY with my girlfriend and the song is so viscerally evokes the feeling of seeing that she really loves new york and i really loved SF.

but i dont seem to be able to know when to hold on or let go in this kind of situation. when ships drift apart how do you know if the rope will pull them back together or snap? and even if the rope is strong enough should it be broken?

i feel like i can't give up on people because it feels like it would mean i could give up on myself. and i feel like i am unresolved about the futures i didn't have because i haven't mourned the person i wouldve/couldve been.

i left both companies basically just because i could tell that being there wasn't the right path for me and working there would've been worse for both parties. but i wonder if i could or should have tried harder to make it work.

i havent really figured out the wanting/having thing but i dont really think about it so much. im mostly thinking about freedom to/from recently. the best answer i have so far is that you kind of have to feel the pain of freedom to, to willingly accept giving up freedom to in order to gain freedom from. i've been lifting more consistently and it makes me much more likely to give up freedom to sleep whenever i want in favor of getting enough sleep so my gym performance doesnt suffer.

im also thinking more about emotions and bodily sensation as the only real ground truth sensor. its very easy to think and spiral and i eventually realized that i could justify anything with enough thought. and also that if i traced back my beliefs far enough it wasnt really clear which ones were original, adopted, or cargo culted. being able to 'resonate' with a belief by feeling it helped more.

speaking of feeling emotions, having a vocabulary for it helps a lot. thinking of them as 1. somewhere in the body and 2. describing them the way i might describe a physical object helped most. thinking about sharp/dull, heavy/light, warm/cold, discrete/diffuse, etc.
